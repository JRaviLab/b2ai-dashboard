Overview

This document provides a comprehensive analysis of Bridge2AI (B2AI) consortium onboarding data, examining team composition, temporal evolution, institutional diversity, and participation patterns across data generation projects (DGPs) and cores. The analysis focuses on understanding the structure and dynamics of this large-scale team science initiative.

Questions to potentially answer

  1. Temporal scope: any aspects that may have temporally evolved.

  2. Career stage classification: still trying to get the best columns for this – but want to gauge their career stage – Trainee/postdoc, early-career (assistant prof), mid-career (associate prof), senior (full prof)? Or based on years since terminal degree?

  3. Success metrics: are we evaluating “successful collaborations” – what are metrics/proxies to consider?

  • Number of cross-DGP collaborations?
  • Participation in multiple modules/cores? (most cores are deprecated)
  • Longitudinal engagement (continued participation)?
  • Publications/outputs (if available elsewhere)? (ask Jake/Sean)
  1. Team composition variables of interest: beyond PIs and affiliations, maybe we can look at:
  • Expertise diversity (based on the expertise field)?
  • Disciplinary backgrounds (from degrees)?
  • Geographic distribution?
  • Core vs. DGP membership patterns?
  1. DGP identification: I see there are columns for Voice, AI-READi, CHoRUS, and CM4AI. Anything else to focus on?

Questions for teaming members

  1. Success metrics: What specific outcomes define “successful collaboration” in B2AI? Are there milestone completion data, deliverable tracking, or quality metrics already being collected? (in addition to our milestone doc)

  2. Career stage refinement: Do we have access to more detailed career information (academic ranks, years of experience) to improve career stage classification?

  3. Longitudinal tracking: Are there plans to track participants over time (tall ask?)? Understanding who stays engaged vs. who leaves would be valuable.

  4. Publication/output data: Maybe we can publication records, GitHub contributions, or dataset generation be linked to this onboarding data? (w/ Sean, Casey’s work?)

  5. Network data: Are there communication logs (email, Slack), meeting attendance, or other interaction data that could reveal actual collaboration patterns?

  6. Survey integration: Maybe link team science evaluation surveys be linked to this onboarding data for richer analysis?

  7. Specific hypotheses: For the commentary paper, what specific team composition questions are most relevant? E.g.:

    • Does institution type diversity predict better outcomes?
    • Do cross-DGP participants serve as effective bridges?
    • What career stage mix optimizes team performance?
    • Does geographic diversity enhance or complicate collaboration?

  8. Privacy considerations: For publication, should all institution names be anonymized, or is it acceptable to identify top contributors?
